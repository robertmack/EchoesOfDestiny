# Remembering — Design Vision

## Core premise

The game is about a forgotten world that gradually learns to remember itself.

A player inhabits a single awakened character for a day, guiding that character through work, exploration, crafting, farming, and survival. At the end of the day, the world resets, but the character retains memories, skills, and routines. Repeated actions leave behind echoes of permanence, and over many days the world becomes easier to live in, more connected, and more dangerous.

## Core pillars

- Memory: the awakened character remembers previous days.
- Echoes: repeated effort leaves traces that gradually become permanent.
- Routines: the player teaches a character how to live a day by recording a sequence of semantic jobs.
- Whispering: the character can help crops and other things remember how to grow.
- Civilization: small efficiencies compound into a world that slowly becomes more capable.

## The player experience

The player is not a permanent hero. The player is a temporary inhabitant of one life at a time.

The player starts by living one day in a broken homestead, gathering sticks, stones, grass, and wild grain, crafting a crude hoe, planting wheat, tending the field, harvesting, cooking, eating, and sleeping. The next day, the player can replay that routine, then refine it.

## Design philosophy

- The world exists without the player.
- The player does not merely gain power; they change how the world remembers.
- Friction is the main enemy. Echoes reduce friction over time.
- Progress is not just stronger stats. It is reclaiming time, making the day easier, and letting a civilization become more efficient.
- The game should feel like a simulation of lived experience, not a checklist of quests.
